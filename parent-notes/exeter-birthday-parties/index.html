BIRTHDAY PARTIES / GETTING STARTED

“Can I have a birthday party?”

Children’s birthday parties in Exeter: Where do
I start?

A party. A plan. One thing at a time.

Research notes by Yewon
Exeter & Devon · Checked 20 October 2026

I’m beginning to plan a children’s birthday party in Exeter. Before choosing a venue, I’m gathering the questions that matter: who’s coming, what’s included, and what I’ll need to organise myself. These are notes from the planning stage — not a review of a party we’ve already held.

01

THE PEOPLE & THE PLAN

Who’s coming?

Before opening another venue tab, I want to start with the kind of time my child would enjoy. Running around with a group? Making something together? Or spending time with just a few favourite friends?

A rough guest list

Note the children’s ages and likely numbers, plus any adults who may stay. Ask the venue about siblings and supervision rather than assuming everyone can come.

A whole-party budget

Include the venue, activities, food and cake, plus any extras you choose. Keep a refundable damage deposit separate from the cost you expect to pay.

A couple of possible dates

Check the available slots against your family’s plans. Ask the school whether it has guidance about handing out invitations; there isn’t one invitation rule established by this guide.

My starting point: a party that suits our child — and an amount of organising that works for our family.

02

TWO WAYS TO FIND A SPACE

Where shall we have it?

First, separate a venue’s party package from hiring a room. A low headline hire rate may leave food and activities to organise; a package may still have extras to add. Neither is automatically the better fit.

03

THE DETAILS THAT HELP IT COME TOGETHER

What do I need to prepare?

Invitations

Date, start and finish times, address, how to RSVP and whether a parent should stay. Ask guests to tell you about dietary or access needs.

Food & cake

Check the menu, outside-food rules and cake arrangements. Ask what is provided: plates, cups, candles, serving tools and food storage. Discuss allergies directly with the provider.

Before and after

Allow for setting up and clearing away. Check payment dates, final guest numbers, cancellation terms, cleaning and rubbish rules.

ONE LAST NOTE / THE BUDGET

A few ways to keep costs down.

I’d start with the memberships we already have, then compare the whole bill — not just the price of the room.

Local benefits worth checking

  • iPlay & iBounce Exeter: iBelong advertises 10% off parties. Membership costs £10 a year per household, so subtract the joining fee from any saving and check that your chosen package qualifies. Official benefits ↗
  • Wonford: a three-hour weekend children’s party slot is advertised at £75, or £67.50 for members. Ask about membership eligibility and any membership fee before comparing totals. Official rates ↗
  • Crealy: existing Platinum Annual Pass holders have discounted birthday packages listed as a benefit. Ask for the applicable quote; no discount percentage is specified on that page. Official benefits ↗

Small choices, a clearer total

  • Ask whether another date or time would cost less.
  • Add food, accompanying adults, entertainers, decorations, delivery and any cleaning charges to the venue quote.
  • Keep decorations and party bags optional, rather than adding them automatically.
  • Before buying a voucher, check that it covers birthday parties, your chosen date, its expiry and refund terms.

A sale is only a saving if it suits the party we actually want.
